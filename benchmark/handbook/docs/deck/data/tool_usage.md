## Findings

- **Call volume differs ~4x across models.** Claude Opus and Sonnet are the most economical (16–23 calls/trial in every arm). GPT-5.6-sol (40–80) and Gemini-4-argon (34–58) use the most calls. Haiku uses 22–34 and GPT-6 sol/luna use 30–50. The extra calls are mostly reads and executePython: Gemini-3.8-flash averages about 14 executePython calls/trial in A0.
- **Without policy tools, everyone reads the SOP.** In A0 and R, ≥92% of trials of every model open the SOP document (1.1–1.9 SOP-file calls/trial). Gemini-3.8-flash re-reads it most, at about 4.5/trial. With policy tools (A2), raw-SOP access falls to 7–12% of trials for the Claude models but stays at 24–68% for GPT/Gemini, which use both sources.
- **Every model uses policy tools in A2, but at very different volumes.** At least 90% of A2 trials make a policy call. Claude Opus and Sonnet make only 2.5–5 policy calls/trial, and these mostly replace inbox/file reads (Opus A2 21 calls vs A0 20). GPT-6, Gemini and GPT-5.6-sol make 15–27/trial, which adds 15–30 calls/trial on top of A0. GPT models favour `policy__procedure` (8–10/trial), while Gemini favours `policy__read` (6–9/trial, full text). On Haiku, A1-code makes the most policy calls (13.9/trial vs 10.4 for A1-text and 9.4 for A2).
- **Interwhen cuts exploration, not actions.** R reduces total calls relative to A0, most for the high-volume models (gpt-5.6-sol 51→40, gemini-4-argon 48→34, opus 20→16, sonnet 21→17), and nearly all of the cut comes from reads. Writes stay flat at about 5–13/trial, and outbound email/Slack messages stay at about 4–7/trial across all models and arms.
- **Interwhen blocks an action in about 25–52% of trials.** That is 0.34–1.23 pre-tool denials per trial; GPT-6-luna is highest (1.23/trial, 52% of trials) and Gemini-4-argon/A2+R lowest. Completion denials (refused stops) occur in 7–33% of trials. Adding the A2 structure slightly lowers pre-denials for most models but raises completion denials for GPT-6 sol/luna (18→27% and 23→33% of trials).
- **Most blocked actions are fixed.** For Claude and Gemini, 79–93% of denied actions are later followed by an allowed call of the same tool. GPT-5.6-sol is the outlier at 54–64%: it tends to reroute rather than retry. Refused stops are almost always resolved, except for GPT-6-sol A2+R (41% resolved, 12% of trials hit the denial budget), GPT-6-luna A2+R (63%) and Haiku (59–67%).
- **Our A3 gate has the opposite profile to interwhen.** It rarely blocks actions (9% of trials, 0.10/trial) but refuses a stop in 43% of trials (0.82/trial), and every refused stop is resolved.
- **Trials with denials score lower** (e.g. Haiku R 0.81 vs 0.87; GPT-6-sol A2+R 0.78 vs 0.88). Denials are a marker of harder or riskier trials; this is confounded, not a causal effect of the gate.

Caveats: test and dev are pooled. Opus has two test attempts per task (sweep and sweep2) and its R-dev job was still being written, so only part of it is included (16 trials at last run). Gemini-3.8-flash has test only. The sweep was still running at extraction time (2026-11-02 07:2x UTC). SOP-file access is a heuristic: a syntara call whose arguments mention 'sop'.
